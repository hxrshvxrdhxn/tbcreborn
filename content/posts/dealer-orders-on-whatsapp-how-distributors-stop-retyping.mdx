If you sell through dealers, you probably know the morning routine. Orders arrive as phone calls, WhatsApp messages, photos of handwritten lists and the occasional email. Someone in the office reads each one, works out what the dealer meant, checks their price and credit, and types it into the system. Then they call back to confirm.

It works, but it is slow, it depends on one or two people, and it makes mistakes at exactly the moments you are busiest.

## What it really costs

- **Office time.** A distributor with a hundred active dealers can spend several hours a day just entering orders.
- **Errors.** "Same as last time" and "20 boxes of the 4-inch" are open to interpretation. Wrong items and wrong prices lead to returns and credit notes.
- **Missed scheme sales.** A dealer five boxes short of the next scheme slab rarely knows it. Neither does your salesperson.
- **Credit surprises.** Orders get processed for dealers who are over their limit, and the problem is found at dispatch.

## Why dealer portals often fail

Many companies have tried a dealer portal and seen it ignored. The usual reasons are simple: it needed a laptop, it asked for too many fields, it did not show the dealer's own prices, or it took longer than a phone call. Dealers will use something that is faster than calling the office, and nothing else.

## What dealers will actually use

The ordering experiences that work share a few traits:

1. **It works on the phone, in under a minute.** The dealer sees their usual items and reorders in a few taps.
2. **It shows their own prices and schemes.** Not a public price list, but what that dealer pays, with any running scheme applied as they order.
3. **It tells them how close they are to the next slab.** "Add 5 more boxes to qualify for the next scheme slab" is useful to the dealer and good for you.
4. **Credit is checked on the spot.** The dealer sees what is available before submitting, so there is no awkward call later.
5. **It shows dispatch status and dues,** which removes a large share of the "where is my material" calls.

## Spot the dealers going quiet

There is one more benefit people rarely expect. Once orders flow through one system, you can see when a regular dealer stops ordering. A dealer who has not ordered in six weeks is often talking to a competitor. A call from your salesperson at that point is worth far more than one after they have switched.
