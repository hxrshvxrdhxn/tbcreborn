Every company that sells instruments, valves, pumps, electricals, chemicals or engineered products has two or three people everyone goes to. Which model handles 0–10 bar in SS316? What is the maximum temperature for this valve? Does this grade meet that standard? The answers are almost always in the catalogues, datasheets and manuals. Finding them is the hard part.

So the questions go to the senior engineers, all day, by phone and WhatsApp. They answer patiently, and their own work waits.

## Why "just search the PDFs" does not work

Technical documents are not written to be searched. The answer to a selection question is usually in a table, split across columns, in units the person asking did not use. A sales engineer asking about 150 psi will not find a datasheet written in bar with a keyword search. New hires do not know which of forty PDFs to open.

General chat tools do not solve this either. They answer fluently, but a confident answer with the wrong pressure rating is worse than no answer at all. In technical sales, a wrong number becomes a wrong quote, a wrong product and an unhappy customer.

## What a trustworthy answer looks like

For technical teams, an answer is only useful if it can be checked. That means:

1. **It quotes the source.** Every answer shows the document and the page it came from, so the engineer can open it and confirm.
2. **Numbers match the source.** Ranges, limits and ratings are checked against the page, not paraphrased.
3. **It says when it does not know.** If the documents do not cover a question, the right answer is "not covered", with the nearest relevant pages, and the question goes to an expert.
4. **It understands units.** A question in psi finds a datasheet in bar, and the answer shows both.

## The gaps become a to-do list

A useful side effect is that every unanswered question shows you where your documentation is thin. Over a few weeks, the list of "not covered" questions tells you exactly which datasheet to update or which application note to write.

## Your experts get their time back

When routine questions are answered from the documents, with the page shown, the senior engineers only see the questions that genuinely need them. Sales replies go out faster, and new hires get up to speed without waiting for someone to be free.
