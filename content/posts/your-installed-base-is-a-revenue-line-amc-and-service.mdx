Ask most equipment manufacturers how many machines they have in the field and you will get a confident number. Ask where each one is today, who the contact is, when its warranty or AMC ends, and what was replaced on the last visit, and the answer usually becomes "let me check".

That gap matters, because for many OEMs the installed base is the most dependable source of revenue they have. Every machine sold is a stream of service visits, spares and contract renewals, as long as someone is looking after it.

## How service revenue leaks away

The leaks are quiet and they add up:

- **AMCs lapse without anyone noticing.** The renewal date passes, the customer does not chase it, and the contract moves to a local technician.
- **Warranty work is done for free after the warranty has ended,** because nobody checked the date before the visit.
- **Engineers arrive without the right part.** The fault history was in someone's notebook, so the first visit is a diagnosis and the fix needs a second trip.
- **Spares are sold reactively.** Consumables and wear parts are only ordered when something breaks, instead of on a schedule you could have planned.

None of these is dramatic on its own. Over a year, across a few hundred machines, they are often the difference between a service team that costs money and one that makes it.

## What a proper installed-base record contains

For every machine you have sold, you want one record with:

1. **Where it is now,** including the site address and the current contact, not the buyer from five years ago.
2. **Warranty and contract dates,** with renewals flagged well before they are due.
3. **Service history,** covering faults, visits, parts replaced and readings taken.
4. **Spares and consumables** linked to the model, so you know what each machine will need and when.

With that in place, service can be planned instead of reacted to.

## Make it easy for the customer to raise a call

One small change makes a big difference: a QR sticker on every machine. The customer's operator scans it, describes the problem, and the call is raised against the right machine with its history attached. The response clock starts at that moment, and your team can see it.

## Send the engineer prepared

Before a visit, the engineer should see the machine's last faults, the parts replaced and the likely part needed this time. That turns more visits into first-time fixes, which customers notice and remember at renewal time.
